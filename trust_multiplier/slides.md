# Lencioni's Pyramid of Dysfunctions

<div class="flex justify-center mt-4">
<div class="w-full max-w-xl flex flex-col-reverse">

<v-click>
<div class="bg-red-500 bg-opacity-40 p-4 text-center rounded-b-lg">
  <div class="font-bold text-xl">Absence of Trust</div>
  <div class="text-sm">No one's vulnerable, everyone's guarded</div>
</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="bg-yellow-500 bg-opacity-30 p-3 text-center mx-8">
  <div class="font-bold">Fear of Conflict</div>
  <div class="text-xs">Problems don't get discussed, they fester</div>
</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="bg-green-500 bg-opacity-30 p-3 text-center mx-16">
  <div class="font-bold">Lack of Commitment</div>
  <div class="text-xs">People nod in meetings but don't buy in</div>
</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="bg-blue-500 bg-opacity-30 p-3 text-center mx-24">
  <div class="font-bold">Avoidance of Accountability</div>
  <div class="text-xs">No one calls out poor performance</div>
</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="bg-purple-500 bg-opacity-30 p-3 text-center rounded-t-lg mx-32">
  <div class="font-bold">Inattention to Results</div>
  <div class="text-xs">Individual egos win over team outcomes</div>
</div>
</v-click>

</div>
</div>

<v-click>

<div class="text-center mt-4 text-black-500">
Every level is caused by the one below it. <span class="text-red-500 font-bold">It all starts with trust.</span>
</div>

</v-click>

<!--
SCRIPT: Look at this pyramid. When there's no trust at the base, people are afraid to have honest conflict. When people avoid conflict, they don't actually commit to decisions — they just pretend to. When there's no commitment, no one holds each other accountable. And when there's no accountability, results suffer. Every single dysfunction traces back to trust. Fix the foundation, and the rest gets easier.
-->

# What It Looks Like in Practice

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-8 mt-8">

<div>

### 🔴 Low Trust Team:
<v-clicks>

- Sprint planning takes forever — no one commits
- Retrospectives are quiet or performative
- People escalate to you instead of resolving peer issues
- "That's not my job" culture
- Delivery is unpredictable

</v-clicks>

</div>

<div>

### 🟢 High Trust Team:
<v-clicks>

- Commits and delivers with less supervision
- Retros surface real problems — and solutions
- Peer accountability is the norm
- People help each other without being asked
- Velocity is consistent and improves

</v-clicks>

</div>

</div>

<!--
SCRIPT: Let me make this concrete. In a low-trust team, you feel it. Sprint planning is a negotiation. Retros are quiet. People come to you with conflicts instead of resolving them. In a high-trust team — the team delivers. The retro is actually useful. People hold each other accountable so you don't have to do it all yourself. That's the difference trust makes in your day-to-day life as a manager.

# Charles Green's Trust Equation

<v-click>

<div class="text-center mt-8 p-8 bg-gradient-to-r from-blue-500/20 to-purple-500/20 rounded-xl">

<div class="text-4xl font-mono">

Trust = <span class="text-green-400">(C + R + I)</span> / <span class="text-red-400">S</span>

</div>

</div>

</v-click>

<v-click>

<div class="grid grid-cols-4 gap-4 mt-8 text-center">

<div class="p-4 bg-green-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">
  <div class="text-2xl mb-2">C</div>
  <div class="font-bold">Credibility</div>
</div>

<div class="p-4 bg-green-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">
  <div class="text-2xl mb-2">R</div>
  <div class="font-bold">Reliability</div>
</div>

<div class="p-4 bg-green-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">
  <div class="text-2xl mb-2">I</div>
  <div class="font-bold">Intimacy</div>
</div>

<div class="p-4 bg-red-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">
  <div class="text-2xl mb-2">S</div>
  <div class="font-bold">Self-Orientation</div>
</div>

</div>

</v-click>

<!--
SCRIPT: Trust equals Credibility plus Reliability plus Intimacy — divided by Self-Orientation. The numerator is what you build. The denominator is what destroys it. Let me break each one down.
-->

# Breaking Down The Equation

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-6 mt-6">

<v-click>
<div class="p-6 bg-blue-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">
  <div class="text-2xl mb-2">💡 Credibility</div>
  <div class="font-bold">"Do I believe what you say?"</div>
  <div class="text-sm text-black-500 mt-2">
    Do you know your domain? Does your judgment hold up? Are your assessments accurate?
  </div>
</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="p-6 bg-green-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">
  <div class="text-2xl mb-2">⏰ Reliability</div>
  <div class="font-bold">"Can I depend on you?"</div>
  <div class="text-sm text-black-500 mt-2">
    Do you follow through on commitments? Do you show up? Can your team count on you?
  </div>
</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="p-6 bg-purple-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">
  <div class="text-2xl mb-2">🤝 Intimacy</div>
  <div class="font-bold">"Do I feel safe with you?"</div>
  <div class="text-sm text-black-500 mt-2">
    Can people bring you bad news? Do they feel heard? Do you create space for honest conversation?
  </div>
</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="p-6 bg-red-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">
  <div class="text-2xl mb-2">🎯 Self-Orientation</div>
  <div class="font-bold">"Whose interests come first?"</div>
  <div class="text-sm text-black-500 mt-2">
    Are you protecting your team — or your own metrics? Is your ego in the room?
  </div>
</div>
</v-click>

</div>

<!--
SCRIPT: Credibility — do people believe your decisions and your expertise? Reliability — do you do what you say you'll do? Intimacy — do people feel psychologically safe with you? They bring you the real problems, not the sanitized version? And Self-Orientation — this is the killer. If people sense you're managing upward, protecting yourself, or playing politics — trust collapses. Even if you're credible and reliable.
-->

</v-click>

<v-click>

### In Practice:

<div class="text-sm mt-4">

- "I made a mistake in that decision. Here's what I learned."
- Acknowledging uncertainty in planning sessions
- Sharing your own working style — including your weaknesses

</v-click>

<v-click>

<!--
SCRIPT: The fastest way to build trust as a leader is to go first. Vulnerability. When you admit you don't know something, or that you made a mistake, you give everyone else permission to do the same. That is how psychological safety gets built — not through a values poster on the wall, but through watching their manager be human.

# 3. Working Agreements

<v-click>

<div class="text-2xl mb-6">
Make the <span class="text-blue-500 font-bold">implicit</span> explicit.
</div>

</v-click>

<v-click>

<div class="p-4 bg-orange-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg mb-6">
Every team has unwritten rules. The problem is, <span class="text-orange-500 font-bold">new people don't know them</span> — and even veterans disagree about what they are.
</div>

</v-click>

<v-click>

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-4">

<div class="p-4 bg-green-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">

### Example Agreements:

- "Be on time to ceremonies"
- "Hold yourself and others accountable"
- "Be transparent — no surprises"
- "Assume good intentions"
- "Raise blockers early"

</div>

<div class="p-4 bg-blue-500 bg-opacity-20 rounded-lg">

### Key Points:

- Not rules from above
- Team creates them **together**
- Revisit them **every quarter**
- Write them **down and display them**

</div>

</div>

</v-click>

<!--
SCRIPT: Working Agreements are the team's social contract. And here's the critical part: you don't write them for the team. You facilitate the team in writing them together. When people create the rules themselves, they own them. They enforce them on each other. That peer accountability is exactly what Lencioni said you need — and it only exists when trust is there. Working Agreements help create the conditions for that trust.
-->
